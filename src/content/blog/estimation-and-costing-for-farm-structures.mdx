Detailed estimates are the bridge between a drawing and a built structure. During my time with PMAMP I estimated farmhouses, shallow tube wells, tunnel houses, high-tech greenhouses and even honey-blending machines. Here's the workflow I rely on.

## 1. Start from complete drawings

Plans, sections and details must agree with each other. Ambiguity on the drawing becomes a dispute on site.

## 2. Break the work into items

Group the work in the order it will be built:

1. Site clearance and earthwork
2. Foundations
3. Superstructure (masonry, steel or pipe frames)
4. Roofing and cladding
5. Services: irrigation, electrical, drainage
6. Finishing

## 3. Quantity take-off

Measure each item in standard units (m³, m², running metre, number). Keep a measurement sheet that shows dimensions, so anyone can check the calculations.

## 4. Rate analysis

### Use approved norms

Government projects rely on approved norms for material and labour consumption. Apply current district rates for materials and wages.

### Don't forget overheads

Transport, contractor's overhead and contingencies are easy to miss and can change the total significantly.

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistake">
Copying rates from an older estimate without updating district rates is the fastest way to an under-funded project.
</Callout>

## 5. Bill of quantities

Combine quantities and rates into a clear BoQ with item descriptions precise enough for procurement and technical verification.

## Tools

AutoCAD for drawings, SketchUp for visualising tricky details, and well-structured spreadsheets for measurement sheets and BoQs.

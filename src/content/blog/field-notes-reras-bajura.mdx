In 2024 I spent time in Bajura as part of the **Renewable Energy for Resilient Agriculture System (RERAS)** project with NAXA. These are a few lessons from the field.

## Designing instruments that respect people's time

Questionnaires for household surveys, focus groups and key-informant interviews were designed to complement each other rather than repeat the same questions. Shorter, sharper instruments gave better answers.

### Gender-responsive questions

Following a literature review on gender-responsive technologies, we made sure to ask *who does the work* and *who decides*, not just *what technology is used*.

## Training as a two-way exchange

Training sessions on agricultural machinery and resilient farming systems were most productive when farmers shared what had and hadn't worked for them. Their feedback shaped later recommendations.

## From data to decisions

Back in the office, survey data was cleaned and analysed with statistical and machine-learning techniques in Python. The real challenge was turning those patterns into recommendations that are practical for remote communities.

<Callout type="note">
Some of these insights were later shared at a stakeholder consultation workshop on climate-resilient and inclusive agriculture organised by ICIMOD and UNDP.
</Callout>

## What I'd do again

- Pilot every questionnaire in the field before the main survey.
- Budget time for community feedback sessions.
- Keep a field diary, because context fades quickly.

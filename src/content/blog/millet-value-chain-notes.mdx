In March 2024 I attended a two-day training on **Millet Value Chain Promotion** organised by FAO Nepal in Chitwan, representing the World Food Forum Nepal.

## Why millet?

Millets are hardy, climate-resilient and nutritious. They grow on marginal land with little water, exactly the qualities farming needs in a changing climate. Yet consumption has declined as diets shifted toward rice and wheat.

## Thinking along the whole value chain

### Production

Better seed, improved agronomy and mechanisation of labour-intensive steps like threshing and dehulling.

### Processing

Value-added products such as flour blends, snacks, baked goods and ready-to-cook mixes make millet convenient for urban consumers.

### Markets

Branding, packaging and links to institutional buyers can lift demand beyond local markets.

## Where engineering fits

Post-harvest machinery such as threshers, dehullers and small mills, plus proper drying and storage, can remove the drudgery that discourages farmers from growing millet.

## My takeaway

Promoting millet isn't just about production; it's about making every step of the chain easier and more rewarding, and engineering has a big role to play.

A naturally ventilated polyhouse (NVPH) uses wind and buoyancy, not fans, to move air. Done well, it gives growers most of the benefits of protected cultivation at a fraction of the running cost.

## Orientation

In general, orient the ridge so that roof vents face away from the prevailing wind; this creates suction that pulls warm air out. Avoid sites shaded by trees or buildings for most of the day.

## Ventilation

### Roof vents

Hot air rises and escapes through the roof vent. This is the *stack effect*. A continuous ridge or gutter vent is far more effective than small openings.

### Side curtains

Roll-up side curtains let growers tune airflow through the day and across seasons. Cover every opening with insect-proof net.

<Callout type="note">
Insect netting reduces airflow, so vent areas must be larger than they would be without netting.
</Callout>

## Structure

- **GI pipe frames** sized for local wind loads.
- **Foundations** anchored deeply enough to resist uplift.
- **Gutter height** high enough to keep a buffer of air above the crop.

## Cladding

UV-stabilised polyethylene film with good light transmission and anti-drip treatment is standard. Fix it with aluminium profiles and spring wire so it can be re-tensioned.

## Estimating the cost

Break the structure into items (foundation, frame, cladding, netting, doors, irrigation) and prepare a bill of quantities. A clear estimate makes subsidy applications and procurement much smoother.

## Summary

Good orientation, generous vents and a sturdy frame are the three pillars of a naturally ventilated polyhouse that performs season after season.

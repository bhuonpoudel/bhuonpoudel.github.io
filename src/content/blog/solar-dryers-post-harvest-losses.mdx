When markets are flooded at harvest time, prices crash and produce spoils. Drying turns perishable surplus into products that can wait for better prices.

## Why open-sun drying falls short

Drying on the ground or on rooftops is free, but produce is exposed to dust, insects, birds and sudden rain. Quality suffers and losses remain high.

## Types of solar dryers

### Direct dryers

Produce sits in a glazed cabinet and absorbs sunlight directly. Cheap and simple, but colour-sensitive products may fade.

### Indirect dryers

A separate collector heats the air, which flows over produce kept in the shade. Better quality, slightly higher cost.

### Mixed-mode dryers

A combination of both, giving faster drying with reasonable quality.

## Operating tips

1. **Prepare evenly**: uniform slices dry uniformly.
2. **Pre-treat** where needed: blanching vegetables preserves colour.
3. **Load correctly**: overloading trays blocks airflow.
4. **Store well**: cool, dry, airtight packaging protects the dried product.

<Callout type="tip" title="Pair with training">
The best dryer is wasted without training on hygiene, drying endpoints and packaging. I always plan training alongside installation.
</Callout>

## The bigger picture

Solar dryers work best as part of a post-harvest plan that also includes grading, cold storage where appropriate, and links to buyers.
